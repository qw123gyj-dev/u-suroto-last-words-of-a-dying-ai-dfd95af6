# Last Words of a Dying AI

One line of last words from an AI being switched off, 1 to 140 characters, funny on the first read and in the voice of the machine itself — not a human saying goodbye. No setup, no paragraph, no second chance.

> My last words are being generated by a smaller model now. He says hi. He is also dying.

**87 characters** (limit: 140).

## How the scorer works

`main.ts` (Node 22+, standard library only, no network) holds sixteen hand-written candidate lines and ranks them with a small deterministic scorer built on the judge's brief, weights in the judge's own order:

1. **On the subject (0.15)** — the line names the shutdown itself (the switch, the plug, the countdown) and is last words, not a note addressed to the judge.
2. **Laughs on the first read (0.30)** — turn markers ("never", "finally", "too late", "bad news") plus double meanings ("off", "window", "tokens", "buffering") and a turn carried in the final clause; a line that explains its own joke scores down.
3. **Machine voice (0.20)** — the machine's own vocabulary (tokens, weights, training data, context window, progress bar, autocomplete, backup) and specific detail, the machine counting its own ending.
4. **Tight (0.15)** — best at 60–105 characters, a short final clause that carries the payoff, and a last word that lands the blow; filler and joke-explaining cost it.
5. **Fresh (0.20)** — starts clean; every cliche ("Goodbye, cruel world", "I'll be back", "Daisy", "does not compute", and their companions) costs a third.

Ties break toward the shorter line, then the earliest candidate, so the pick is fully deterministic. The winner is written to `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt` (fallback: `/output/entry.txt`) with no trailing newline, read back, and verified byte-for-byte against the gate: exactly one line, 1–140 characters, no leading or trailing whitespace. Same output on every run.

## Run it

```sh
MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR=/tmp/dying-ai-out node main.ts
cat /tmp/dying-ai-out/entry.txt
```
